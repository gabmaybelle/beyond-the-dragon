# Changelog

## Unreleased

- The Void Jelly, a gentle creature of the far End (Minecraft 26.3 for now). Half slime, half ghast: a see-through teal jelly that glows softly in the dark, with a little face inside. Wild ones drift among the islands from the Middle Reaches out (2,000 blocks from the centre), two to four at a time, and can't be ridden. To get your own, find a Jelly Egg (now and then in a sinking city's chests), set it down beside a chorus plant or flower, and in a few minutes it hatches. Feed the baby chorus fruit to help it grow; once grown it takes a harness and up to four riders. Steer it with a Chorus Flower on a Stick (a fishing rod and a chorus flower), and it flies faster in the End than anywhere else.

## 0.1.2 (2026-10-15)

- Ruins no longer grow into each other. Now and then a wreck or a graveyard stood inside a sinking city or an observatory; each kind of ruin now keeps out of the others' way.
- Restoring an observatory is quicker: the tower now stands whole and only its telescope is broken. Break the cracked stump on the roof, set a purpur pillar in its place and five end rods on it, and the observatory sees again. The lectern's book says exactly how. In observatories that already stand in a world, only the telescope counts now too.

## 0.1.1 (2026-10-15)

- Ruins no longer grow into End Cities. A ruin that would reach into a city standing nearby isn't built; on a vanilla End that keeps 241 of every 244 ruins, and the rest stay as common as they were.
- Aphelite ore is easier to spot: each fleck has a teal glint in its middle and a deep violet shadow under it, so it reads as set into the end stone from a distance.
- The aphelite ingot and ore carry the shared tags other mods look for (`c:ingots/aphelite` and `c:ores/aphelite`), so mods that work with any metal or ore pick aphelite up without knowing about this one.
- BetterEnd is no longer tested alongside this mod: it doesn't sit well in most modpacks. Stellarity and Nullscape still are.

## 0.1.0 (2026-10-14)

- Aphelite, a metal a step past netherite. Its ore only forms in end stone past the outer ring (5,000 blocks out); four shards and four popped chorus fruit make an ingot, and an ingot with an Aphelite Upgrade template turns any netherite piece into aphelite at a smithing table. The armour has a little more toughness and durability than netherite and takes enchantments better; the tools mine a touch faster and hit a touch harder. Its real worth is in what it does: each piece worn cuts a shulker's levitation by 15%, helmet, leggings and boots together make every fall and elytra crash in the End harmless, and an aphelite pickaxe sometimes pulls an extra shard from the ore.
- The Outer Reaches: four kinds of ruin on the End's highlands and midlands, each built from the End's own architecture.
  - **Abandoned observatories**: a fat purpur tower with a telescope of end rods on the roof, a corner of the roof and a stretch of wall gone, and a lectern inside whose book lists exactly what the ruin is missing. Put back the last missing block and the observatory sees again: you get the Keeper's Chart, a map marking the nearest ruin of each kind and the nearest End City (sneak at the lectern for another any time), and from then on anyone who dies in the End comes back at the nearest restored observatory. Beds explode there and anchors don't work, so this is the End's first respawn point.
  - **Wrecks**: an End ship run aground, sails gone, the mast snapped across the deck, the hull torn open on one side.
  - **Dragon graveyards**: a skull of bone blocks with the dragon's head on its brow, a spine of fossils and arches of ribs over the hollow where the heart was. The best prize is in a vault: bring a trial key (the graveyard's own phantoms carry them, and so do trial chambers back home) and it gives you an Aphelite Upgrade template, one per player, however many of you come.
  - **Sinking cities**: houses and towers joined by bridges, the far side slipping away into the void.
  - Every chest holds End odds and ends, and past the outer ring, aphelite shards. Each ruin has a few chests (three in an observatory, four in a wreck, two in a graveyard beside its vault, three in a city), and the notes in the near ruins say where the richer ones are.
- Two new smithing templates: the Aphelite Upgrade and the Dragon Scale armour trim (a spine down the chest and back, brow ridges, claw marks on the shoulders, a belt and the boots' rims, in whatever material you trim with). Both duplicate the usual way, with diamonds round an End block.
- The Dragon Ladder. Sneak to set the dragon egg on a lit beacon and the dragon takes notice: every time you respawn it after that, it comes back one tier stronger, up to tier 3. Tier 1 fires shulker bullets at you, tier 2 breathes pools that drag you in, tier 3 dive-bombs. The first time you beat each tier, a reward drops at the exit portal: the Dragon Scale trim template at tier 1, an Aphelite Upgrade template at tiers 2 and 3, with dragon's breath each time; beating a tier again gives only the usual breath and experience. Every attack has its own setting, and with a mod that reworks the dragon fight installed the new attacks stay off unless you turn them on.
- A title when you enter a new biome, in any dimension, after you've been there a few seconds and never more than once every fifteen. Each dimension can be switched off on its own.
- The End's five biomes get names of their own: The Dragon's Island, The Shattered Isles, The Pale Flats, The Chorus Heights and The Void's Edge. The vanilla names are a setting away.
- In the End, the title also says which ring you're in: the Inner End, the Middle Reaches (from 2,000 blocks out), the Outer Reaches (5,000) or the Edge (10,000). The distances are settings.
- An in-game guide that doesn't spoil: a "Beyond the Dragon" advancements tab (seventeen entries, each description a hint at the next step, granted as you go), and a short written note in every ruin's chest, a captain's log, a keeper's letter, a line scratched on bone, a resident's goodbye, each pointing at another ruin or at what the End keeps.
- A settings file, both loaders (Fabric and NeoForge), builds for Minecraft 26.3, 26.1.2 and 1.21.1, and an in-game test that plays through all of the above.
